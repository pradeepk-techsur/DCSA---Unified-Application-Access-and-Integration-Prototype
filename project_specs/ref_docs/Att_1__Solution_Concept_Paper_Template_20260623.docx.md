Problem Understanding

Vendors shall clearly describe their understanding of DCSA’s operational problem and the desired end-state for the warfighter or user.

Proposed Concept & Vision

Vendors shall provide a one-page narrative describing their "big idea." It should focus on the unique approach, the user experience, and the strategic value it will deliver.

Visuals like a simple concept diagram are encouraged.

High-Level Technical Approach

Vendors shall briefly outline their technical philosophy.

Vendors shall explain why they recommend a certain technology stack, architectural style (e.g., modular, open-source), or development methodology (e.g., Agile, DevSecOps) for this specific problem.

Intellectual Property & Data Rights

Vendors shall state their proposed data rights and licensing posture as follows:

Background IP: Identify any pre-existing software or algorithms delivered as proprietary background intellectual property and state the specific licensing terms and user rights granted to the Government to run, integrate, and maintain this background IP.

Foreground IP: List the proposed rights and license terms granted to the Government for any custom software, code adapters, interfaces, or configurations developed under this effort.

Dependencies: Disclose any third-party software, libraries, or data feeds required to sustain the prototype.

Risk & Opportunity Spotlight

Vendors shall identify the single biggest technical or programmatic risk that could jeopardize the project.

Vendors shall identify the single biggest opportunity the government might be overlooking with this project.

Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM)

Vendors shall provide a high-level cost estimate for a potential prototype project, broken down into major phases if applicable (i.e. project phase / estimated duration / estimated cost range).

Discuss any cost share (if applicable); the Government may note any issues or questions for further clarification before requesting a full proposal.
